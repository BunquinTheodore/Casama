Parcels arrivewhen nobody is in

What 95,736 real orders, plus weather and holidays, say about late deliveries

Casama helps housemates coordinate who receives a parcel. This page asks when parcels turn up late, using real marketplace orders, daily weather from Open-Meteo and public holidays from Nager.Date. It is the Data Science final project of group Theoraux.

01

The question

A parcel that arrives late is a parcel that may arrive when nobody is home. Casama's parcel board tracks incoming couriers, who is in, and cash on delivery owed. If we knew early which parcels tend to run late, the board could warn housemates sooner.

So we ask when parcels arrive late, and whether weather or holidays help explain it. These three questions were written down before we looked at any result.

  1. Parcels that see more heavy rain days at their destination in the 7 days after the seller hands them to the carrier are late more often.
  2. Parcels with a national holiday in that same window are late more often.
  3. Adding weather and holiday facts makes a late parcel model more accurate than one that only knows when the seller handed the parcel over.

Each question is answered whichever way it turns out.

Weather is taken at one point per state, its capital, so it is a coarse stand in for the weather along a parcel's real route. The orders cover purchases from January 2017 to August 2018. They are Brazilian orders, not Philippine ones, and no file says which courier carried a parcel or whether it was cash on delivery.

03

Cleaning

We started from 99,441 orders. 2,965 were never delivered. Of the 96,476 with a delivery date, we removed 740 with impossible or extreme timings: a step recorded before the purchase, a delivery time of zero or less, a journey longer than two months, or a purchase from before January 2017. That leaves 95,736, which is 96.3% of everything.

What counts as late

A parcel is late if it arrives on a later calendar day than the estimated date. Estimates are stored at midnight, so judging by the clock would label 1,290 parcels that arrived on the promised day as late. Under that clock rule the late rate would be 7.85% instead of 6.51%.

What the data shows

05

Weather and holidays

For each parcel we look at the 7 days starting on the day the seller hands it to the carrier. A heavy rain day is a day with at least 10 mm of rain at the destination state capital, following the ETCCDI R10mm climate index. Holidays are nationwide public holidays. Intervals on late rates are ninety five percent bootstrap intervals. Adjusted odds ratios account for the month of purchase and the state, and their ninety five percent intervals allow for parcels handed over in the same week being alike, so they are wider than a plain fit would give.

Heavy rain

No clear effect. The raw gradient disappears once month and state are taken into account. Each extra heavy rain day changes the odds of a late parcel by a factor of 1.01 (interval 0.95 to 1.07), which cannot be told apart from no effect. Rain on its own did not explain late parcels here.

Public holidays

The opposite of our guess. Parcels with a holiday in their window were late less often: 4.9% against 6.8%, an adjusted odds ratio of 0.55 (interval 0.44 to 0.67). We did not test why, so we make no claim about the cause.

06

Can we predict late parcels

We trained a gradient boosting model on parcels bought before January 2018 (43,285 parcels) and tested it on 52,450 later parcels it had never seen. Three versions know more and more: one at purchase, one after the seller ships, and one that also knows the weather and holidays after handoff.

ModelWhat it knowsAUC
At purchaseDays promised, weekday, hour and month of the order0.552
After shippingEverything above plus how long the seller took to hand over the parcel0.625
With weather and holidaysEverything above plus heavy rain days, total rain and a holiday flag for the window0.661

AUC measures how well a model ranks late parcels above on time ones. A coin toss scores one half and a perfect model scores one.

Improved. Adding weather and holiday facts raised the AUC from 0.625 to 0.661, a gain of 0.036 (interval 0.031 to 0.041). Of the added signals, total rain in the window mattered most (importance 0.079). We did not test whether the gain mostly reflects where parcels were sent or the time of year, because weather differs by state and by season, so read it as a modest association.

The model is modest. Flagging the riskiest parcels catches 27.1% of the late ones, with 18.1% of the flags correct, against a late rate of 7.5% in the test months. The weather here is observed after the fact, so this explains parcels that already arrived. It is not a forecast the app could use today.

07

Takeaways

  1. About 6.5% of parcels arrive after the estimate. Most arrive well before it.
  2. The seller's handoff time is the strongest warning sign: 19.6% late when the seller is slow, against 3.1% when it is fast.
  3. Heavy rain looks like a cause in the raw numbers, but the pattern disappears once month and state are taken into account.
  4. A public holiday in the window went with fewer late parcels, the opposite of our guess, and we did not test why.
  5. A model can beat guessing but stays modest: an AUC of 0.66 at best.

08

From findings to Casama

These are ideas for the parcel board, not features built in this project.

  1. Ask for the seller's handoff time when a parcel is added to the board, and warn housemates early when it is slow, since that is when parcels run late.
  2. Treat any lateness warning as a nudge to plan who will be home, not as a promise, because the model is modest.

Reuse in another country

The holiday call used here works for any country by changing one code. Asked for PH in 2026, the Nager.Date API returned 18 public holidays, starting with New Year's Day, Chinese New Year, Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Holy Saturday. Weather works the same way by changing the coordinates, so the pipeline can be repurposed for Philippine households.

09

What this does not prove

  • The data is Brazilian marketplace data, not Philippine, so it says nothing certain about Casama's households.
  • It holds timestamps only. There is no courier, payment type or cash on delivery amount.
  • Weather is one point per state (27 capitals), and the seller's location is unknown, so weather is only measured at the destination.
  • These are associations, not causes. We did not test why parcels with a holiday in their window behaved as they did.
  • Parcels without a carrier handoff date (1 of 95,736) are left out of the weather and model results.
  • The model is modest and should be treated as a nudge.

Definitions, references and credits

Late parcel
A parcel delivered on a later calendar day than its estimated delivery date.
Estimated date
The delivery date promised to the customer at purchase.
Handoff
The moment the seller gives the parcel to the carrier.
Window
The 7 calendar days starting on the handoff date, the same for every parcel.
Heavy rain day
A day with at least 10 mm of rain at the destination state capital.
Odds ratio
How much the odds of being late change. One means no change, above one means more often late, below one means less often.
Interval
A range the true value is likely to lie in, here ninety five percent. If the interval of an odds ratio spans one, the effect cannot be told apart from none.
AUC
How well a model ranks late parcels above on time ones.
